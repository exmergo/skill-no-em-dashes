The microwave beeped. Neither of them moved toward it.

"All I'm saying is, if you'd looped me in before sending the deck to."

"I did loop you in," Dana said. "I sent the draft Tuesday."

"You sent it at six. I was already."

"At six, which is a workday."

Marcus set his coffee down too hard. A little sloshed over the rim. "Let me finish a sentence. One sentence. If you'd given me the actual."

"The actual what? It was all there." She folded her arms. "You skimmed it and now you're."

"See, that. That right there." He pointed at the space between them. "You do that. You decide what I'm going to say and then you."

"Because I already know what you're going to say."

The silence stretched. Somebody's lunch turned slow circles behind the glass.

"Fine." Marcus's voice dropped. "Then say it for me. Go ahead."

Dana opened her mouth. Closed it.

"That's what I thought," he said.

"Don't," she warned. "Don't make this about."

"About what?" He waited. He actually waited this time.

She didn't fill the gap. The microwave beeped again, patient, and they both stood there listening to it.

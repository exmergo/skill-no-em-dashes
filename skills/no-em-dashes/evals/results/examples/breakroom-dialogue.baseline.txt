The microwave hummed between them. Dana stirred her coffee and didn't look up.

"Look, all I'm saying is that if you'd just sent me the file before the—"

"Before the meeting, yeah, I've heard this already."

"No, you haven't, because if you had, you'd understand that I couldn't present numbers I didn't—"

"You always do this." Marcus set his mug down too hard. Coffee jumped over the rim. "You make it sound like one person dropped the ball when actually—"

"I'm not saying one person. I'm saying I needed—"

"You needed me to read your mind, apparently."

Dana stopped stirring. The spoon clinked once against the ceramic and went still.

"Can I finish a sentence? Just one. Can I get to the end of one sentence in this conversation?"

Marcus opened his mouth, then closed it.

"I needed the file," she said slowly, "because Patterson asked me directly, and I had nothing to give him, and I looked like I didn't know my own project."

The microwave beeped. Neither of them moved to open it.

"That's not what I—" Marcus started.

"I know," Dana said, and walked out, leaving her coffee on the counter.
